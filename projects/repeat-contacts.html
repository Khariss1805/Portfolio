Finding where repeat contacts come from

The desk reported FCR from an agent checkbox. I measured it from what customers actually did: did they contact us again within seven days?

54.9%of repeats come from two reasons60%of repeats happen within 48 hours

This is a practice project. The company is fictional and the data is synthetic, generated to behave like real contact-centre data. The questions, methods and code are the ones I would use on the job.

The question

Agents marked contacts as resolved, so reported FCR was above 90%. Volume kept growing anyway.

The question: how often do customers really come back, about what, and how fast?

The data

26,000 contacts over six months, with customer ID, timestamp, channel and contact reason.

What I did

  1. Used window functions (LEAD over each customer’s contacts) to find the next contact by the same customer within seven days.
  2. Counted a contact as a repeat only if the next one was about the same reason, so a new problem is not counted against the agent.
  3. Grouped repeat rate by reason and by days until the customer came back, and published it in Tableau.

What I found

Real FCR is 79.9%, not 90%

20.1% of contacts are followed by another contact about the same thing within a week. Delivery (32.1%) and product faults (27.5%) have the highest repeat rates.

Customers come back fast

60% of repeat contacts happen within two days. That points to answers that did not solve the problem, such as tracking information that was not up to date, rather than new problems.

What I would recommend

  • Replace the checkbox with the 7-day repeat measure in every report.
  • Review delivery and product-fault answers in the knowledge base first. Bringing them to the desk average would avoid about 52 contacts a week.
  • Send a proactive update within 24 hours for open delivery cases.
