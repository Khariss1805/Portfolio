Did the new checkout design really work?

The design team simplified the checkout from four steps to two. Before rolling it out, I checked whether it changed what customers did.

+25%more completed purchases+28%on mobile, where it mattered most

This is a practice project. The company is fictional and the data is synthetic, generated to behave like real product data. The questions, methods and code are the ones I would use on the job.

The question

A fictional online shop redesigned its checkout in Figma: fewer fields, a progress bar and guest checkout on the first screen.

The question: does the new design make more people finish their purchase, and is the effect the same on mobile and desktop?

The data

48,578 checkout sessions over 21 days, split at random between the current design (A) and the new one (B), with device and whether the purchase was completed.

What I did

  1. Checked the split first: if A and B did not get about half the traffic each, the test would be broken.
  2. Compared conversion rates with a two-proportion z-test and confidence intervals.
  3. Broke the result down by device, because the redesign mainly targeted small screens.
  4. Plotted conversion day by day to make sure the result was stable and not an early fluke.

What I found

The new checkout converts better

Conversion went from 2.91% with the current design to 3.64% with the new one, 25% more. The difference is very unlikely to be chance (p < 0.001).

Mobile users gain the most

On mobile, conversion rose from 2.46% to 3.14% (+28%). Desktop improved too, by 21%. Fewer fields matter most on a small screen.

What I would recommend

  • Roll out the new checkout to everyone, starting with mobile.
  • Keep watching conversion for a month after launch, in case the effect fades once it is no longer new.
  • Next test: remove the account creation step on mobile entirely.
