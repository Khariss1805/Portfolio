Forecasting weekly orders for an online store

The store ordered stock and planned campaigns by gut feeling. I built a forecast they can update every week, and showed when customers actually buy.

5.1%average forecast error41%of orders placed between 19:00 and 23:00

This is a practice project. The company is fictional and the data is synthetic, generated to behave like real product data. The questions, methods and code are the ones I would use on the job.

The question

Stock ran out every November and piled up every February.

The question: how many orders will arrive in the coming weeks, and when in the week should campaigns go out?

The data

Two years of weekly orders, and order timestamps by weekday and hour.

What I did

  1. Built a seasonal forecast: the same week last year, adjusted by year-on-year growth.
  2. Tested it honestly: forecast the last 12 known weeks as if they were unknown, then compared with what happened.
  3. Added a likely range around the forecast, so planners see the uncertainty and not just one number.
  4. Mapped orders by weekday and hour to time email and social campaigns.

What I found

A simple model is accurate enough

On the 12 test weeks the average error (MAPE) was 5.1%. Next week the store should expect about 2,362 orders, rising 23% at the year-end peak.

Customers buy in the evening, and on Sundays

41% of orders arrive between 19:00 and 23:00, and Sunday alone brings 18% of the week. Campaigns sent at 9:00 on a weekday miss most buyers.

What I would recommend

  • Order stock for the year-end peak from the forecast’s upper range, not the middle.
  • Send campaigns around 18:30 on weekdays and on Sunday afternoon.
  • Update the forecast weekly and track its error, so planners learn to trust it.
