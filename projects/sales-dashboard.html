A sales dashboard an online store can run on

The store tracked sales in five spreadsheets that never agreed. I built one model and one dashboard, so every team reads the same numbers.

53.8%of revenue from two categories21.9%of apparel orders returned

This is a practice project. The company is fictional and the data is synthetic, generated to behave like real product data. The questions, methods and code are the ones I would use on the job.

The question

A fictional online store sells across six categories. Each team exported its own numbers and meetings started with arguments about which spreadsheet was right.

The question: where does revenue come from, when does it peak, and what is quietly eating into it?

The data

Twelve months of orders: 39,709 orders, their items, products and categories, plus every return.

What I did

  1. Wrote one SQL query per business definition, for example revenue excluding cancelled orders, so every visual uses the same logic.
  2. Modelled the data as a star schema: orders and returns as facts; dates, products and categories as dimensions.
  3. Built the dashboard below: four KPI tiles, monthly revenue and a breakdown by category.
  4. Added DAX measures for average order value and growth against last year.

What I found

Two categories carry the store

Electronics and home and living bring in 53.8% of revenue. A bad month in either one moves the whole business.

December is worth almost two normal months

December revenue is 73% above the monthly average, and the last quarter brings in 35.5% of the year. Stock and campaigns need to be ready by October.

Apparel returns are a hidden cost

21.9% of apparel orders come back, against 8.6% across the store. That is about €89 thousand in refunded sales a year.

What I would recommend

  • Retire the team spreadsheets and use the shared model as the single source of numbers.
  • Plan stock and marketing for the last quarter from October, not November.
  • Test a better size guide on apparel product pages and track the return rate as its success metric.
