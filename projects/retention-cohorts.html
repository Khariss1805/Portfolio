Does a welcome call keep new members longer?

A membership service started calling new members in their first week. I measured whether it changed how long they stayed.

+16.9 ptsactive members at month 6+7 ptsmonth-3 retention after the rollout

This is a practice project. The company is fictional and the data is synthetic, generated to behave like real contact-centre data. The questions, methods and code are the ones I would use on the job.

The question

Many new members leave in the first month: 24% of email-onboarded members are gone after month one.

The question: do members who get a welcome call stay longer, and is the effect visible in the cohorts since it started in May?

The data

Nine monthly signup cohorts (January to September), each member’s active months, and onboarding channel.

What I did

  1. Built a cohort table in SQL: for each signup month, the share of members still active after each month.
  2. Compared cohorts before and after the welcome call started in May.
  3. Compared retention curves of members with a welcome call and members with email only.
  4. Designed the cohort view in Figma first so it would read clearly before building it in Tableau.

What I found

Cohorts since May stay longer

Month-3 retention averaged 69.7% for January to April cohorts and 76.7% for cohorts from May on: 7 points more.

The gap grows over time

By month 6, 68.3% of members with a welcome call are still active, against 51.4% with email only. Most of the difference is created in the first month.

What I would recommend

  • Make the welcome call standard for all new members, scheduled in the first week.
  • Add a second check-in at the end of month one, where most members leave.
  • Keep a small email-only group for a few months, to measure the effect cleanly.
