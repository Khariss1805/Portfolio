Testing two prototypes of a booking app

A fitness studio wanted an app to book classes. I tested the first Figma prototype, redesigned the weak spots, and tested again with numbers to compare.

80.8SUS score, up from 58.837%less time to complete the tasks

This is a practice project. The company is fictional and the data is synthetic, generated to behave like real product data. The questions, methods and code are the ones I would use on the job.

The question

The first prototype looked good in reviews, but nobody had watched real people use it.

The question: can people complete the five key tasks, where do they struggle, and did the redesign fix it?

The data

Two rounds with 10 participants each. For each of 5 tasks: whether they completed it and how long it took. After each session, the 10-question System Usability Scale (SUS).

What I did

  1. Wrote realistic task scenarios, for example “Your Thursday class is cancelled. Book another one this week.”
  2. Ran moderated sessions on the Figma prototype and logged success, time and comments in a sheet.
  3. Scored SUS in Python and compared both rounds task by task.
  4. Redesigned the two weakest flows, cancelling and changing payment, before the second round.

What I found

Cancelling a booking was the main problem

In the first round only 40% of participants managed to cancel a booking; the option was hidden in a menu. After moving it onto the booking card, 70% succeeded.

The redesign made everything faster

Cancelling went from 136 to 52 seconds, and the five tasks together took 37% less time. The SUS score rose from 58.8, below the usual average of 68, to 80.8.

What I would recommend

  • Build the second prototype and keep cancellation on the booking card.
  • Run a short third round on the invite flow, which did not improve.
  • Track the same tasks in the live app with analytics, to see if the lab results hold.
