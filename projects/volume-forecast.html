Forecasting contact volume and staffing by the hour

The desk staffed the same number of agents all day, every day. I forecast how many contacts would arrive and how many agents each hour really needs.

5%average forecast error76 hoursof agent time saved on a Monday

This is a practice project. The company is fictional and the data is synthetic, generated to behave like real contact-centre data. The questions, methods and code are the ones I would use on the job.

The question

Customers waited too long in the late morning, while agents were idle in the evening.

The question: how many contacts will arrive in the coming weeks, and how many agents are needed each hour to answer 80% of them within 20 seconds?

The data

Two years of weekly contact volume, an intraday profile by weekday and hour, and an average handle time of 380 seconds.

What I did

  1. Built a seasonal forecast: the same week last year, adjusted by year-on-year growth. Simple enough to maintain in Excel.
  2. Tested it honestly: forecast the last 12 known weeks as if they were unknown, then compared with what happened.
  3. Spread the weekly forecast over days and hours using the intraday profile.
  4. Used Erlang C to calculate the agents needed each hour for an 80/20 service level, and checked the Excel result against Python.

What I found

A simple model is accurate enough

On the 12 test weeks the average error (MAPE) was 5%. The forecast for next week is 5,895 contacts, rising to 7,188 in the year-end peak.

Most contacts arrive in two waves

Volume peaks around 11:00 and again mid-afternoon, and Monday is the busiest day.

Staffing by the hour frees real time

At the 11:00 peak, 137 contacts need 19 agents. Staffing for the peak all day would use 247 agent hours on a Monday; following the curve needs 171.

What I would recommend

  • Build schedules from the hourly plan, with staggered start times around the late-morning peak.
  • Use the freed evening hours for training, QA reviews and back-office work.
  • Update the forecast every week and track its error, so planners trust it.
