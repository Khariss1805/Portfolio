Where new users drop out of sign-up

A web app had plenty of visitors but few active users. I followed each visitor through sign-up to find exactly where they gave up.

12.8%of visitors become active users48%of mobile users lost at email verification

This is a practice project. The company is fictional and the data is synthetic, generated to behave like real product data. The questions, methods and code are the ones I would use on the job.

The question

A fictional project-management web app gets 40,000 visitors a month, but only a small share ever create their first project.

The question: at which step do people leave, and is it the same on mobile and desktop?

The data

One month of event logs: each user’s device and the sign-up events they reached, from viewing the landing page to creating a first project.

What I did

  1. Turned raw events into one row per user with a flag for each funnel step, using conditional aggregation in SQL.
  2. Calculated the share of visitors reaching each step, and the share lost at each step, by device.
  3. Built the funnel in Tableau and shared it with the design team.
  4. Sketched a lighter verification flow in Figma for the step that lost the most users.

What I found

Mobile users fall behind at every step

18.7% of desktop visitors end up creating a project, against 9.5% on mobile. The gap opens at email verification.

Email verification is the step to fix

After people start signing up, verification is the biggest leak: 39.1% of users who enter their email never confirm it. On mobile it is 48%, on desktop 26%. People switch to their email app and do not come back.

What I would recommend

  • Let users start working before verifying their email, and ask for it later.
  • On mobile, replace the confirmation link with a six-digit code users can type without leaving the app.
  • If mobile verification matched desktop, the app would gain about 1,050 active users a month.
